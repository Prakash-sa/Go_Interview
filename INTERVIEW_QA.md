# Go concurrency interview cheat sheet

- **Channels vs mutex** — Q: When would you pick channels over a `sync.Mutex`? Trade-off: channels shine when ownership can be serialized through a goroutine (no shared state), mutex is simpler for short critical sections; channels can model backpressure but add goroutine + channel overhead.
- **Buffered vs unbuffered channels** — Q: Why/when to buffer a channel? Trade-off: buffering decouples producer/consumer and encodes queue limits; pick a capacity tied to downstream throughput. Unbuffered enforces rendezvous (synchronization) but can stall a fast producer.
- **WaitGroup vs errgroup** — Q: How do you coordinate goroutine lifecycles? Trade-off: `sync.WaitGroup` is minimal and low-overhead; `errgroup.Group` adds cancellation + first-error propagation. Both need Add before launch and `Done()` in each goroutine.
- **Signaling completion** — Q: How do you broadcast “we’re done”? Trade-off: closing a channel unblocks all receivers (fan-out signal) without data races; a `bool` with mutex requires extra plumbing; contexts give deadline + cancellation propagation.
- **Select + cancellation** — Q: How do you avoid goroutine leaks in pipelines? Trade-off: every blocking send/receive should also listen on `ctx.Done()`; otherwise downstream can hang forever. Prefer one ctx per request over scattered `time.After`.
- **Worker pool bounds** — Q: How do you cap concurrency? Trade-off: buffered-channel semaphore or fixed worker pool. Semaphores are compact for independent tasks; pools help when you need shared setup per worker. Both must drain/close to avoid leaks.
- **Backpressure** — Q: How do you prevent a producer from overwhelming consumers? Trade-off: bounded buffers (drop/block), rate limiters (`time.Ticker`), or demand signals (request/ack). Dropping loses work but keeps latency; blocking preserves work but can stall.
- **Channel closing semantics** — Q: Who should close a channel? Trade-off: only the sender; receivers should range until closed. Avoid sending on a closed channel (panic). Use a dedicated `done` close for broadcast instead of sentinels where possible.
- **Context usage** — Q: Where do contexts belong? Trade-off: pass `context.Context` as the first arg, derived from the caller. Avoid storing contexts in structs or using `context.Background()` deep inside libraries; respect deadlines to keep systems responsive.
- **Testing goroutines** — Q: How do you test concurrent code deterministically? Trade-off: prefer dependency injection (tickers, channels), use short timeouts, and expose hooks to wait for goroutines (WaitGroup) instead of sleeping arbitrarily.
